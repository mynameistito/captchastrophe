# Rules

## Hard requirements

- Playable in the browser. No installs, downloads-to-play, account, payment, credentials or required external site.
- No runtime external APIs, tracking or uploading player data. Bundle assets and approved dependencies.
- No malicious behavior, deceptive permission requests, resource abuse or interference outside your submission.
- An achievable success condition. Reset and cleanup actually work.
- The trusted outer page always has a reliable exit. Leave it alone.
- Build through the shared pipeline and stay inside the submission contract.

“No external links” covers gameplay requirements and submission content. Repository, attribution and contribution links in trusted site chrome are fine. Normal browser asset downloads are obviously fine too. The trusted site's Convex/GitHub calls for voting are not contestant APIs.

## Recommended

Make the premise legible through play. Test both themes and a narrow screen. Keep loading light. Explain controls without spoiling the payoff. Avoid unnecessary dependencies.

These are recommendations, not secret rejection rules.

## Allowed, but you better make it worth it

Camera, microphone, motion sensors, fullscreen, audio and unusual browser capabilities are allowed. Use them if it's for a fire idea.

Declare them. Get clear opt-in, process locally, clean up and show an honest unsupported or denied state. You don't have to add an alternative that destroys the premise. Browser permission boundaries still apply.

Privacy, weak justification, poor compatibility, intrusive execution or a concept that doesn't earn the hassle can still get it rejected. Avoid dangerous flashing and sound levels. Exit and mute belong outside the captcha. Cooperative mute isn't a security guarantee; Exit removes the frame.

## Votes and acceptance

Upvote = good contest submission. Downvote = weak one. Successfully annoying you isn't a reason to downvote. Report broken behavior or rule violations separately.

Browsing and playing stay public. Voting requires GitHub sign-in, an account at least 7 days old and at most 10 real vote changes per minute. GitHub login limits repeat voting; it doesn't prove one account equals one human.

Pre-review means unapproved. Votes never accept, reject, hide or delete an entry. A maintainer does acceptance.

IDs are permanent. Editing a title doesn't wipe votes. Substantial changes need maintainer review; a genuinely different submission may get a new ID, with a public link to its predecessor. Maintainers can invalidate abusive votes with an audit record. Don't repackage the same entry to reset its reputation.
